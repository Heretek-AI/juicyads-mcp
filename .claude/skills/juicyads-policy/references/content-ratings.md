# Website content ratings

Source: JuicyAds help article *Website Content Ratings* (`360034884733`).
Verified 2026-09-02.

Every website on the network carries a content rating that describes the nature of
its content, which images are permissible, and which links are acceptable when
buying ads there. Non-compliance can get an ad rejected or removed by the publisher
**without prior notice**.

## The scale (only three buyable ratings)

### Non Nude
- Ads must contain **no nudity at all**: no hardcore, topless, or explicit images
  showing more than typical FHM/Maxim.
- Rule of thumb: an image that is not explicit is non-nude.
- You **may** promote nude or even hardcore *websites* here — but you must avoid
  explicit/nude *images*.

### Softcore
- A pornographic category that **may feature nudity**, but hardcore images
  (depicting penetration or explicit sexual acts) are **NOT** allowed.
- Images without sexual intercourse or oral sex are generally acceptable.
- Caution: many publishers treat **any image featuring a penis as hardcore** — avoid
  them in softcore ads.
- Links to hardcore content are allowed; explicit images must be avoided.

### Hardcore
- **No restrictions**; general and explicit links and images are all allowed.
- Publishers may still request changes or removal if an ad is deemed obscene or
  inconsistent with the site's content.

## Important implication (verbatim from JuicyAds)

> JuicyAds no longer offers a "General/Non Adult" content rating for websites, and we
> cannot guarantee that only safe-for-work (SFW) content will be displayed in your ad
> zones.

So when classifying a site for ad buying: pick **Non Nude**, **Softcore**, or
**Hardcore**; there is no "General/SFW" option, and never promise a user that an ad
zone will show only SFW content.

## Classifying a site/offer

1. What do the site's *images* show? (explicit imagery is the gating factor)
2. What does the site *link to*? (hardcore destinations are allowed even under
   Non Nude images)
3. When unsure, be conservative: a borderline image counts as the stricter rating,
   and many publishers enforce stricter than the minimum.
